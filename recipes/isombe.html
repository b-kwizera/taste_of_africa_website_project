Isombe

Rwanda's beloved dish of pounded cassava leaves simmered with groundnuts.

About this dish

Isombe is a traditional Rwandan dish of pounded cassava leaves slowly cooked with eggplant, spinach and ground peanuts. The leaves must be cooked for a long time to be safe and tender, producing a deep green, savoury stew. It is usually served with rice, ugali or boiled plantains (ibitoke).

🌍 Rwanda⏱ Prep 30 min · Cook 1 h 15 min

Where it comes from

Ingredients

Servings: 4
  • 500 g cassava leaves, finely chopped (frozen is fine)
  • 1 eggplant, diced
  • 150 g spinach
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic
  • 3 tbsp ground peanuts or peanut butter
  • 3 tbsp palm oil
  • 500 ml water
  • 1 green pepper, chopped
  • 1 tsp salt

Cooking instructions

Show / hide the steps
  1. Boil the cassava leaves in water for 20 minutes, drain, and squeeze out the liquid. Never skip this step: raw cassava leaves are toxic.
  2. Heat the palm oil and fry the onion, garlic and green pepper until soft.
  3. Add the leaves, eggplant and 500 ml water, then simmer covered for 40 minutes.
  4. Stir in the spinach and ground peanuts and cook 10 more minutes until thick.
  5. Season with salt and serve with rice, ugali or plantain.
