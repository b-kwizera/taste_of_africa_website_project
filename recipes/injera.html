Injera with Doro Wat

Spongy fermented teff flatbread used to scoop up spicy Ethiopian stews.

About this dish

Injera is the foundation of Ethiopian and Eritrean meals. This sour, spongy flatbread is made from teff, a tiny ancient grain, fermented for several days. Meals are served on one large shared injera and eaten by hand, tearing off pieces to scoop up stews such as doro wat, a slow-cooked chicken stew seasoned with berbere spice.

🌍 Ethiopia⏱ Prep 30 min + 3 days fermenting · Cook 45 min

Where it comes from

Ingredients

Servings: 4
  • 300 g teff flour
  • 500 ml water, plus extra for the batter
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 6 chicken drumsticks
  • 3 large red onions, finely chopped
  • 3 tbsp berbere spice mix
  • 3 tbsp niter kibbeh or butter
  • 4 cloves garlic
  • 4 boiled eggs

Cooking instructions

Show / hide the steps
  1. Whisk the teff flour with 500 ml water, cover loosely, and ferment at room temperature for 2–3 days until bubbly and sour.
  2. Thin the batter with a little water to a crepe-batter consistency and add the salt.
  3. Pour a thin spiral of batter onto a hot, non-stick pan, cover for 2 minutes. Do not flip. The surface should be full of tiny holes.
  4. For the doro wat, cook the onions dry in a pot for 15 minutes, then add the niter kibbeh, garlic and berbere.
  5. Add the chicken and 250 ml water, simmer 40 minutes, then add the boiled eggs.
  6. Serve the stew on top of the injera.
