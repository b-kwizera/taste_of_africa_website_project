Jollof Rice

A smoky, tomato-rich one-pot rice that headlines every celebration across West Africa.

About this dish

Jollof rice is the pride of West Africa. Nigerians, Ghanaians, Senegalese and many others each claim the best version in the famous "jollof wars". It is cooked in a rich base of tomatoes, peppers and onions, then steamed until every grain is glossy and slightly smoky. No party, wedding or festival is complete without it.

🌍 Nigeria⏱ Prep 20 min · Cook 50 min

Where it comes from

Ingredients

Servings: 4
  • 500 g long-grain parboiled rice
  • 400 g tinned chopped tomatoes
  • 2 red bell peppers
  • 1 large onion
  • 2 scotch bonnet peppers (to taste)
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 80 ml vegetable oil
  • 600 ml chicken or vegetable stock
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 2 bay leaves

Cooking instructions

Show / hide the steps
  1. Blend the tomatoes, bell peppers, half the onion and scotch bonnet until smooth.
  2. Heat the oil in a heavy pot, fry the remaining sliced onion, then stir in the tomato paste for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the blended mix, curry powder, thyme and bay leaves. Simmer 20–25 minutes until the sauce is thick and the oil rises to the top.
  4. Rinse the rice, add it with the stock, and stir once so the rice is coated in sauce.
  5. Cover tightly with foil and a lid, then cook on very low heat for 30 minutes without stirring. Let the bottom catch slightly for the classic smoky flavour.
  6. Fluff with a fork and serve with fried plantain or grilled chicken.
